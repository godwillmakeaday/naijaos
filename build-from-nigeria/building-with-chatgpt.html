Building with ChatGPT: What I Do and What AI Does

Purpose, technical assistance and the decisions that remain mine.

By Paul A. Magaji · 5 min read · Documented collaboration

When I say I use ChatGPT to build websites, the description can hide the most interesting part: how the work is divided.

I bring a purpose and its circumstances. Through conversation, that purpose develops into a structure and something I can inspect. Sometimes I need help organising my thinking; sometimes technical assistance. At other times, I need to explain why a proposed direction misses the point.

Understanding those moments helps me use the tool deliberately.

I bring context

A general request for a property website leaves many possibilities open. A request to organise property observations while making clear that they do not establish ownership sets a more precise task.

Likewise, my interest in Nigerian institutions, power and opportunity gives NaijaOS a particular direction. ChatGPT can develop an idea, but the circumstances help establish why it is worth developing.

I also decide what belongs. When a new section must respect an existing design, I need to make that condition explicit.

AI helps make the work inspectable

A long conversation can become a proposed set of pages, an article sequence or a description of a tool’s behaviour. Seeing the structure lets me identify what I meant and where the interpretation differs.

The fluency of the response can create an early impression of agreement. I therefore need to examine assumptions, scope and emphasis before allowing the proposal to become the foundation for more work.

My corrections are part of building.

Technical help needs actual information

I can ask about code, deployment or errors in ordinary language. That makes unfamiliar work easier to approach.

But a reasonable explanation based on an assumed setup may address the wrong problem. Exact errors, relevant pages, current configuration and earlier changes give the conversation a firmer starting point.

When I understand a little more of the technology, I can also follow the answer more closely and recognise what needs clarification.

I remain responsible for the claims

While preparing this series, a recovered record identified Truehost where I had referred to Bluehost. It also did not establish a successful resolution. Those details changed the article.

A readable account must still be accurate. I need to inspect sources, distinguish facts from possibilities and avoid presenting an unfinished investigation as a completed fix.

The same responsibility applies to what I publish through my projects.

Progress needs a clear description

A page written, a feature implemented, a check passed and a public deployment are different kinds of progress. I want to know which has occurred.

Before deciding the next action, I ask: what changed, what evidence supports it and what remains unverified?

ChatGPT helps me attempt more. My purpose is also to become a better director and maintainer of the work.

A useful instruction to reuse

Ask for a result together with its assumptions, changes made, checks performed and unresolved questions. Then examine the actual work. A confident account of completion needs evidence you can understand.

Case record: preserving NaijaOS while adding a section

One decision from the New Possibilities work is concrete enough to examine. I wanted a section within NaijaOS, with the existing website and its design respected. My instruction concerned the relationship between the new idea and work already in place.

The published homepage and New Possibilities section show the resulting arrangement. Readers can move between the existing operating-intelligence material, the essay collection and the new programme. The worksheet belongs within that wider inquiry.

This illustrates a contribution I bring to AI-assisted work: deciding what the project is becoming and what must be preserved. The implementation then needs to be assessed against that direction. A working page alone would not establish that it fitted the intended project.

A source trail for the collaboration

The New Possibilities review record distinguishes checks performed from checks still missing. It records keyboard interaction and accessibility-tree observations while explicitly leaving actual screen-reader use and real-device testing unresolved.

The collection release is another inspectable output of this collaboration. The editorial direction came from my account and requests; AI assisted drafting, implementation and verification. A repository records changes to files. It does not independently prove who conceived every idea or establish that I personally wrote the code.

I want readers to understand that distinction when assessing my contribution.

What the record changed

During preparation of this series, the recovered troubleshooting account identified Truehost where I had referred to Bluehost. The published article therefore names Truehost and leaves the outcome unresolved. That correction is evidence of an editorial decision; it is not independent verification of the original provider incident.

The same care applies to the Lovable chapter. With no completed experiment established in the available account, it remains an evaluation plan. I would need actual use and a documented result before treating it as a hands-on review.

What I would ask a reader to judge

Look at whether the instruction, implementation and published explanation agree. Examine the limitations and try the relevant task. If a claim exceeds what the evidence establishes, that is a useful criticism of the work.

These project records support an account of how I direct and assess AI-assisted building. They do not establish independent technical certification, commercial success or an evaluation by outside users.
