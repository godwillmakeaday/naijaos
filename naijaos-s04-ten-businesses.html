Ten businesses to start with ₦100k to ₦500k — read after the briefing.

A list of ten paths sized for the entry capital most Nigerians actually have. Each is scored against the three pillar tests — fuel absorption, FX exposure, buffer requirement — and tiered by how well it survives a shock. The ten are not equal. The point is to make the unequal visible.

01The frame

The list this is not.

Most Nigerian "businesses to start with ₦100k" lists are flat — every path presented as an equal opportunity, the differences hidden inside paragraph length and emoji choice. This is not that list.

The pillar briefing — how money actually works in Nigeria — argues that income outcomes in 2026 are determined by exposure, not effort, and that the question worth asking is not which path is profitable but what happens to this path when the next shock arrives. This list applies that frame. Ten paths sized for the same capital band. Each scored on the same three tests. Each placed in the tier its score earns it.

The result: nine paths that survive the conditions named in the pillar — split between a durable tier and a workable tier — plus one deliberately exposed counter-example, named honestly so the framework remains visible. The exposed path is not on the list because it is good. It is on the list because excluding it would let the framework hide.

A path is not a recommendation. It is a position taken on conditions the operator may or may not understand.
02The method

Three tests, one tier.

Every path below is scored on the three structural questions the pillar's § 07 closes with. The score determines the tier. The tier determines how the operator should read the path.

Scoring methodology
T · 01
Fuel absorption. How well the path absorbs a transport-driven cost spike. Strong means costs and prices move together within a week. Weak means cost rises and prices lag for months.
T · 02
FX exposure. How much of the path's cost base is dollar-denominated. Strong means inputs are local. Weak means imports dominate the cost stack.
T · 03
Buffer requirement. How long the path can survive without revenue before the operator borrows or closes. Strong means low fixed costs and short runway needs. Weak means high overhead from day one.
⚠ Verify before acting

Capital ranges and revenue figures below are working ranges drawn from operator interviews and field reports across Nigerian cities in early 2026. They are diagnostic, not prescriptive. Local conditions vary widely; verify against your specific location, supplier prices, and current charge structures before committing capital.

TIER 01 · DURABLE

Paths that absorb the shocks the pillar names.

All three tests pass. Low fuel exposure, no significant FX exposure, low buffer requirement. These are the paths that quietly compound across cycles. They are not the most exciting — they are the most durable.

PATH · 01

Laundry pickup & delivery service.

Service · neighbourhood-scale · low overhead
Fuel
STRONG
FX
STRONG
Buffer
STRONG

The pickup model — collecting laundry from middle-income households, washing at a central point, delivering folded — has rebuilt itself in Nigerian cities as a low-capital service business. The customer pays per kilogram or per bundle. The operator handles logistics on foot or motorbike. The path absorbs fuel because most pickup routes are short and re-priceable weekly; it carries no FX exposure because detergent, water, and labour are all locally priced; and the buffer requirement is low because there is no shopfront and no large inventory.

The model strengthens with two additions: a recurring weekly subscription (smooths the cash cycle) and ironing as a paid surface (lifts revenue per kilogram by 30 to 50 percent). The failure mode is not economic but operational — service quality decays fast if the operator over-promises pickup windows.

Barbing in a rented shop at a fixed location is one path. Mobile barbing — the operator goes to the customer, charges a premium for the convenience — is structurally different and structurally better suited to current conditions. No rent, no shopfront overhead, premium pricing per visit. Equipment (clipper kit, stool, capes, sterilisation kit) sits inside the entry band. The customer base is built through one or two referrals and a WhatsApp number.

The path scales by adding services that travel with the operator — beard treatment, edge-up styling, basic skincare consultation — rather than by adding locations. A skilled mobile barber serving 30 regular middle-income clients on rotation outperforms most fixed-location shops on take-home.

Tutoring at scale — small group, in-home.

One-to-one tutoring is a job. Small-group tutoring — three to six students of the same age and subject, met twice or three times a week — is a business. The economics shift the moment the second student is added: revenue doubles, costs barely move. The operator who runs four small groups (mornings and afternoons, multiple subjects) clears more than most entry-level corporate salaries.

The path is durable because Nigerian middle-class families pay for educational outcomes even when they cut elsewhere. The path requires teaching skill that not every operator has, and it works best when the operator can travel between three or four nearby households rather than scattering across a city.

PATH · 04

Pure-water and sachet-drinks distribution.

Trade · neighbourhood logistics · cash cycle
Fuel
STRONG
FX
STRONG
Buffer
MEDIUM

Buying sachet water and basic drinks at the factory gate, distributing to micro-retailers in a defined area, collecting daily. Margins per sachet are tiny; the volume and the cash cycle are what create the business. The path absorbs fuel because routes are short and pricing adjusts within days; it carries no FX exposure; and the buffer requirement is medium because the operator must front goods to retailers who pay on a 24-to-48-hour cycle.

The discipline rule is simple: never extend credit beyond what one slow week can absorb. Operators who let receivables stretch past forty-eight hours are functionally lending at zero interest, and the cash cycle that made the business work begins to run in reverse.

₦200k – ₦400k

TIER 02 · WORKABLE

Paths that survive with discipline.

Two tests pass; one is a known weakness the operator must manage actively. These paths can compound — but only if the operator builds the architecture that compensates for the exposed test. Skipping that work is what makes them fail.

PATH · 05

Small-batch food vending — breakfast or lunch only.

Food · single-meal model · location-bound
Fuel
MEDIUM
FX
STRONG
Buffer
MEDIUM

The single-meal model — breakfast jollof and stew sold to office workers, or lunch packs sold to a school catchment — is the version of food vending the conditions reward. The full-day buka model carries higher overhead and slower turnover; the single-meal model concentrates effort and capital into a defined daily window.

The fuel test is medium because food prices in Nigerian markets respond to transport spikes within weeks (the pillar's § 03 documents this transmission). The mitigation is to re-price weekly — a discipline most vendors resist because customers complain — and to keep menu items flexible enough that one ingredient becoming expensive does not break the model.

Trade · cold-chain dependent · power-sensitive
Fuel

MEDIUM

Buying frozen chicken or fish in bulk from cold-room distributors, retailing to households at neighbourhood scale. Margins are workable; the operational risk is power. A 24-hour outage with a freezer half-full of chicken is not a setback — it is an existential event that can erase a month of margin in a single afternoon.

The path scores medium across all three tests because some of the supply chain (imported frozen fish, certain chicken cuts) carries dollar exposure, food-price transmission affects pricing, and the cold-chain requires either reliable power or generator/inverter redundancy that adds to fixed costs. The operators who succeed are the ones who treat power architecture as part of the entry capital, not as an afterthought.

Graphic design, copywriting, social media management for Nigerian small businesses. The capital is mostly device-based: a working laptop, reliable data, perhaps subscription software. The path absorbs fuel because client work happens at the operator's location and pricing is per-project.

FX exposure scores medium because most modern design and content tools are dollar-priced (Adobe, Figma paid tier, scheduling tools, AI subscriptions), and a naira shock raises the operator's cost base while client budgets are slow to follow. Buffer requirement is medium because client payments are irregular — sustainable freelancing in this market requires either retainers or a pipeline that produces invoices on a predictable cycle. Operators who chase one-off projects burn out within a year. Operators who convert clients to monthly retainers compound.

Agriculture · feed-cost sensitive · slow cycle
Fuel
MEDIUM

MEDIUM

A small flock of egg-laying birds (200 to 500), housed in a controlled environment, producing eggs sold to local distributors and households. The model is older than most "modern" Nigerian businesses and survives because eggs are inflation-tracking — when food prices rise, egg prices rise with them, sometimes faster.

FX exposure runs through feed (corn and soya derivatives have international price links), and fuel exposure runs through transport from feed mill to coop and from coop to market. Buffer requirement is medium because the productive cycle (point of lay to peak production) takes months — operators who underestimate the runway between capital deployment and first sustained revenue often run out of feed money exactly when birds reach peak production.

Renting out chairs, tables, canopies, basic décor pieces, and sound equipment to small events — birthdays, weddings, naming ceremonies, church gatherings. The asset is the business; once the kit is bought, the marginal cost per event is small. The path scores strong on fuel because rental prices adjust between events.

FX exposure scores medium because some equipment (sound, lighting, certain décor pieces) is imported. The buffer test is the weak point: weekend-concentrated demand means weeks with no bookings happen often, and operators who treat strong weekends as the new baseline collapse during dry quarters. The architecture that fixes this is a buffer reserved specifically for slow months — not a borrowed top-up, not personal savings repurposed, but a working reserve built when bookings are strong.

TIER 03 · EXPOSED · COUNTER-EXAMPLE

The path most lists recommend that the framework flags.

One path, included for honesty. It fails two of the three tests structurally. It can still produce income — but the operator is taking a position on conditions that the conditions themselves do not currently reward. Read this as the framework working, not as advice to avoid the path entirely.

PATH · 10 · EXPOSED

Mini-importation — phone accessories & gadgets.

Trade · imported inputs · naira revenue
Fuel
MEDIUM
FX
WEAK
Buffer
WEAK

Mini-importation — buying phone accessories, gadgets, beauty items, fashion pieces from international platforms and reselling in Nigeria — was the canonical small-capital path of 2020 to 2022. The pillar's § 04 names what changed: the parallel-market spread that made the arbitrage profitable has narrowed sharply since 2024, and the conditions that built the path no longer subsidise it the way they did.

The structural problem is on both axes the framework measures. FX exposure is weak because the cost base is dollar-priced and the selling price is naira-denominated — every shipment is a bet on the rate holding stable between order and sale. Buffer requirement is weak because shipment lead times and customs delays mean capital is tied up for weeks at a time, and a single seized container can erase six months of margin.

The path is not impossible. Operators with a deep understanding of FX timing, customs logistics, and product categories with truly elastic demand still make it work. But it is no longer a path the framework can recommend on its merits — it is a path that requires the operator to accept structural exposure in exchange for the chance of upside, and to budget honestly for the quarters when the structure moves against them.

Honest accounting: this path is on the list because excluding it would let the framework hide. Most "ten businesses to start" lists include it without naming what changed. Naming what changed is the diagnostic.

06Reading the list

What this list is, and what it is not.

This list is a diagnostic tool. It is not a recommendation engine. The right path for any specific reader depends on variables this post cannot see — location, available time, existing skills, family obligations, the state of the operator's buffer before the path begins.

What the list does provide is a frame for evaluating any path against the same three questions. A path that scores three strongs is durable. A path that scores two passes is workable with discipline. A path with two weak scores is not impossible, but the operator must accept that they are taking a structural position rather than choosing a safe path.

The single most useful thing a reader can do after this list is apply the same scorecard to a path that did not appear here — POS, ride-hailing, dropshipping, anything currently being recommended on a Twitter thread or YouTube video. If the framework cannot produce three confident scores for the path, the path is not yet ready to be a position. Either more diagnosis is needed, or the path is being marketed beyond what its conditions support.

The list of ten is not the answer. The scorecard is the answer. The list is just where the scorecard becomes legible.

The next sibling worth reading is the three-month buffer rule, which treats the architecture every workable-tier path requires before it can begin. The path that follows from this list is whichever one matches the operator's actual conditions — read in the light of the briefing, scored against the three tests, entered with the buffer in place.

07FAQ

Common questions this list keeps getting asked.

Q · 01

What is the best business to start with ₦100,000 in Nigeria?

There is no single best business at any capital level. The right path depends on the operator's location, time availability, and ability to absorb a slow quarter. At ₦100,000, the strongest options are service-based: laundry pickup, mobile barbing, tutoring at scale, or basic food vending. Each has low fuel exposure, no FX exposure, and a small buffer requirement — the three properties that determine survival.

Q · 02

Which businesses are most exposed to inflation and FX in Nigeria right now?

Any path with imported inputs and naira-denominated revenue. Mini-importation, phone accessories, electronics retail, and businesses that rely on foreign software or international suppliers all carry high FX exposure. When the naira moves, their cost base moves with it; their selling prices move slower. Margin compresses on every shipment, and a single rate move can erase a quarter of profit.

Q · 03

Can I start a business in Nigeria with less than ₦100,000?

Yes, but the path narrows. Service-based businesses with low overhead — basic tutoring, errand running, content writing for local businesses, mobile services — can launch below ₦100,000. The trade-off is that lower-capital paths usually require more time investment to build to sustainable revenue. Capital and time are substitutes, not separate inputs; the operator who has neither cannot enter the trade through any path on this list.

Q · 04

How long before a small business in Nigeria starts making profit?

For service-based paths in this capital band, three to six months is realistic for breakeven, with sustainable profit emerging from month four onward in well-located operations. Trade and inventory-based paths break even faster on paper but carry higher hidden costs in spoilage, theft, and float requirements that delay actual profit by several months. The headline breakeven figure is rarely the figure that matters; the figure that matters is when net take-home stabilises at a level the operator can rely on.

Q · 05

Why was POS not included in this list?

POS is a path the system treats with its own dedicated playbook because the economics, the float requirement, and the failure modes are specific enough to deserve full treatment. The S/03 sibling — POS economics in 2026 — covers it in detail. Including it as one item in a list of ten would have under-served both the path and the reader considering it.

Q · 06

Should I take a loan to start any of these businesses?

Generally no, and certainly not at the rates available to most first-time small-business borrowers in Nigeria. The buffer requirement that every path on this list carries is meant to be funded from saved capital, not borrowed capital. Operators who launch on debt face the same failure mode the pillar names in § 04: a slow quarter forces the next loan, which outpaces the margin the business can produce. The architecture this list requires begins with the buffer; the buffer cannot be borrowed without changing the path's structural exposure.

The list scored. The next move depends on what was scored.

If a workable path matched, the architecture comes next. If a durable path matched, the buffer rule. If the exposed path was tempting, the FX briefing earns a careful read first.
