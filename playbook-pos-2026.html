Playbook System 01 — Income Execution

The real cost of starting a POS business in 2026

A POS agency is not free money on a stool. It is a cash business with thin margins, real theft risk, and a float that is not yours. This playbook runs the numbers most agents never run — before you commit one naira.

Updated Execution guide · ~7 min Not financial advice

§Who this is for

For
Anyone weighing a POS / agency-banking business as a primary or side income, with modest starting capital and a fixed or semi-fixed location.
Not for
Anyone expecting passive income, or treating the float as personal money. If you cannot keep business cash separate, stop here.
Level
Beginner to early-operator. Assumes no prior agency experience.

§What this helps you avoid

The three failures that close most POS stands are not dramatic. They are quiet: a float eaten by personal and family spending, a margin too thin to survive downtime and charges, and a single theft or fraud event with no buffer behind it. This playbook is built to surface those before they happen.

§Required starting conditions

  • A fixed or reliable location with steady human traffic — foot traffic is the whole business.
  • Starting float you can afford to lock — money you will not need to touch for personal use.
  • A registered SIM, valid ID, and a bank/fintech agency arrangement you have verified directly.
  • A way to keep business cash physically and mentally separate from household cash.
  • Power and network backup — downtime is lost income and lost trust.

    Reality check

    The float is not profit and it is not yours to spend. It is working capital you are holding for transactions. The day you dip into it for a personal emergency is the day the business starts dying — quietly, while still looking busy.

    §Step-by-step plan

    01
    Verify the agency arrangement at source

    Confirm charges, settlement timing, and dispute handling directly with the bank or licensed fintech — not with a recruiter or reseller.

    02
    Cost the location honestly

    Count real foot traffic across different days and hours before committing. A cheap spot with no traffic is the most expensive choice.

    03
    Lock the float

    Decide the float amount and ring-fence it. Separate phone, separate record, separate cash. No household crossover.

    04
    Run the break-even maths

    Find the daily transaction count and average ticket needed to cover charges, running costs, and a reserve. If the number is unrealistic for your traffic, stop.

    05
    Set cash-safety rules before day one

    Cash-out timing, maximum cash on hand, end-of-day reconciliation, and who is allowed to operate the terminal.

    06
    Record every transaction

    Daily reconciliation is non-negotiable. A POS business that is not reconciled nightly is a leak you cannot see.

    §Common mistakes

    !
    Treating the float as savings

    It earns nothing sitting still and disappears when spent. It is inventory, not a cushion.

    !
    Ignoring downtime

    No power, no network, no float = no income, but the costs continue. Budget for the bad days.

    !
    Underpricing the risk of theft and fraud

    Cash businesses attract attention. A single event with no buffer can end the agency.

    !
    Mixing family pressure with the float

    The most common cause of collapse is not the market. It is the request you could not refuse.

    §Survival rules

    The float is sacred. Reconcile nightly. Price the bad days in. Keep family pressure out of the cash.

    §Before you commit · checklist

  • I have confirmed real charges and settlement timing with the bank/fintech directly.
  • I have counted actual foot traffic at my location, on more than one day.
  • My float is money I can lock away from personal and family use.
  • I have run the daily break-even and my traffic can realistically hit it.
  • I have a nightly reconciliation habit and a cash-safety rule set.
  • I have a buffer for theft, fraud, downtime, and disputes.

    • §When not to do this

    Do not proceed if

    Your starting float is borrowed at a rate you cannot service from realistic margins; your location has no proven traffic; or you cannot keep business cash separate from household demands. In any of these cases, the POS business is not an income path — it is an exposure waiting to be triggered.
