Where the readings come from

Sources

naijaOS groups its inputs by type, so you always know whether a figure is official, reported, legal, or a market signal — and how much weight to give it.

§ Official data

NBS
National Bureau of Statistics — inflation, food prices, transport indices, labour and household data.
CBN
Central Bank of Nigeria — exchange rates, policy rate, monetary circulars.
CAC
Corporate Affairs Commission — business registration, company records, filings.
NNPC
Petroleum pricing and supply notices.
NERC
Electricity tariffs and regulatory orders.
Govt portals
Ministries, agencies, and official gazette channels.

§ Reported data

Reputable Nigerian newspapers and business publications, used for events and context and identified as reported — not as primary measurement. Where reported figures and official releases differ, the official release governs.

§ Legal & institutional

Statutes
Acts of the National Assembly and state laws relevant to everyday risk.
Regulations
Subsidiary legislation and agency rules.
Gazettes
Official notices and commencement dates.
Agencies
Regulators, commissions, and enforcement bodies.

§ Market signals

Fuel
Pump prices and supply conditions, watched as a price-setting mechanism.
FX
Official and parallel exchange-rate movement and its pass-through.
Food
Staple and perishable price movement by market and region.
Transport
Fare and logistics-cost indicators.

User caution

Every figure on naijaOS must be checked against the current official release before you rely on it. Data moves; a number that was right last quarter can be wrong this week. Where this site shows indicative placeholders, treat them as direction, not measurement. When in doubt, go to the primary source.

On linking. naijaOS points to source types and named institutions rather than embedding figures it cannot keep current. For any specific number, consult the named institution’s latest release directly. How we handle sourcing →
