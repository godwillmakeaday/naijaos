# Building from Nigeria for the World

I live in Nigeria, but I do not believe the value of my work should end at Nigeria’s borders.

That conviction is behind my growing interest in websites, artificial intelligence, programming and the platforms through which people publish and build. I want to understand how someone working from here can create something useful enough for people elsewhere to read, use, trust and eventually pay for.

How does a person establish a presence through which their value can travel?

An idea can remain in a notebook for years. A skill can remain known only to friends. Someone may possess a useful perspective but have no dependable way for others to discover it. I have become interested in the distance between possessing something valuable and making it available.

## Give the work somewhere to live

A website allows me to organise what I am building. An article can sit beside the project it explains. A guide can connect to a worksheet. Different pieces can accumulate until a visitor begins to understand the thinking behind them.

I practise law, think about institutions and ask questions about enterprise and opportunity. A passing conversation rarely gives those interests enough room. A website allows me to develop their connections over time.

Someone can encounter an essay and examine a project without first knowing me personally. That is the international presence I want: a growing body of work people can inspect.

## The tools become practical

Once I started building, apparently distant questions became immediate. Where should the website live? How do I connect a domain? Why does a page behave differently on a phone? How do I correct something after publication?

Those questions have led me into hosting platforms, website editors, AI and programming. Each tool earns its place through the task it helps me accomplish.

AI has made me willing to attempt projects that once seemed harder to approach. I can discuss an idea, inspect a first version and work through difficulties. I still need to assess the result and take responsibility for what I publish.

## Reach is an opening

Being online does not guarantee an audience. An audience does not guarantee income. People need a reason to return, and buyers need a reason to trust the work.

My ambition is to create value that can justify a fair price internationally while I work from Nigeria. That requires useful work, clear communication and dependable delivery. The website gives that ambition somewhere to begin.

This series follows my attempts: the choices, costs, mistakes and discoveries. Where an experiment remains planned, I will identify it as such. Where the record does not establish a result, I will leave the question open.

I am building from here. I want the work to travel.

# When Connecting a Domain Consumes Your Day: My Truehost Experience

I wanted people to visit LandDesk through landdesk.ng. The project was on Vercel; the domain needed to connect to it. I expected a finishing task. Instead, it became a troubleshooting exercise that absorbed much of my day.

I changed settings, sought assistance and checked again. At one point, the system displayed “Command completed successfully.” Yet I still lacked confirmation that visitors would reach the intended website.

The experience taught me to ask what an apparent success actually establishes.

## Correct the provider first

During the earlier discussion, I referred to Bluehost. The recovered troubleshooting record identified the registrar interface as Truehost. This article therefore concerns that Truehost-related interaction, rather than a verified Bluehost experience.

The distinction matters. A useful account needs to identify the service accurately. It also reveals a learning difficulty: the registrar, website host and DNS service can occupy different places in the same project. Calling all of them “the host” obscures where a problem belongs.

## What the record establishes

The troubleshooting explored nameserver settings and DNS records. Attempts included Vercel nameservers and an arrangement involving default Cloudoon nameservers and an A record. There was also a DNS-zone creation step.

These are historical attempts, not instructions for another website. I have deliberately omitted an old IP address because copying an unverified historical setting would not establish a correct configuration today.

At one stage, the browser displayed DNS_PROBE_FINISHED_NXDOMAIN. I reported that nameserver settings appeared to revert. Support indicated that updates had been applied, but the result I needed remained unclear.

The record does not establish why this happened or confirm a successful resolution of that session.

## The cost of uncertain progress

I considered transferring the domain because the difficulty was consuming time and I found the support experience frustrating. I wanted a manageable way forward.

The problem was increasingly one of knowing the current state. Which configuration was active? Had the requested change persisted? Was I examining the same arrangement that support was describing?

An assurance and an observed result seemed difficult to reconcile. More changes would not necessarily resolve that uncertainty.

## What I would preserve next time

I would keep a clear record of the starting configuration, each intervention and the observation afterwards. That would make the discussion easier to follow and help avoid confusing a requested change with a confirmed one.

AI helped me discuss unfamiliar terms and possible explanations. Its usefulness still depended on identifying the provider correctly and checking the actual system.

The outcome I wanted was a visitor opening landdesk.ng and reaching LandDesk. Everything else was a step towards it.

## A question for your own investigation

When a service reports success, write down exactly what it has confirmed. Then identify the separate observation that would establish your intended outcome. Keep both in the record.

# How I Approached a Domain Connection Problem—and What I Would Do Differently

When landdesk.ng would not connect as intended to my project on Vercel, I wanted a solution quickly. The more uncertain the result became, the more tempting it was to try another change.

Looking back, I would spend more time establishing the state of the system before the next intervention.

This is a companion to my Truehost account. The recovered record does not establish a completed fix. It offers lessons about organising an investigation while learning.

## Describe the failure precisely

“The website is not working” leaves several possibilities open. Does the project fail everywhere? Does its deployment address open while the custom domain fails? Do different versions of the address behave differently?

I would begin by recording the address checked, the exact result and the time. During my investigation, the browser displayed DNS_PROBE_FINISHED_NXDOMAIN. Preserving that message gave the conversation something concrete to examine.

I would also record whether the separate deployment address worked. That helps identify which part of the journey needs attention.

## Map responsibility

The original conversation involved confusion between Bluehost and Truehost. Correcting the provider was necessary before attributing the difficulty.

I would explicitly identify the registrar, deployment service and active DNS manager. Then I would establish which settings each controlled.

That map would help me ask for assistance in the right place. It would also make it easier to recognise when a proposed change belonged to a different configuration.

## Give each intervention a purpose

Before making a change, I would record the current settings and answer three questions:

- What observation makes this change relevant?
- What result would support the explanation behind it?
- What would I investigate if the result stayed the same?

Afterwards, I would distinguish what the interface accepted from what an independent observation confirmed. If several changes happened before another check, the result would be harder to interpret.

## Make support requests answerable

A useful request identifies the goal, domain, observed failure and relevant changes already attempted. It then asks a precise question about the current state.

I would preserve the replies alongside my observations. If an assurance and the visible result differed, I could point to the discrepancy rather than beginning the explanation again.

Screenshots may help, but they should exclude passwords, verification codes and private account information.

## Define completion

For my purpose, completion required the intended domain to reach the intended project. I also wanted enough understanding to maintain the arrangement.

Until that was verified, I should describe an investigation or attempted fix. A successful command message alone would not settle the larger question.

## A reusable troubleshooting record

For your next technical problem, keep these fields together: goal; exact failure; current setup; proposed explanation; change attempted; observed result; unresolved question. This records a method without pretending that one historical configuration is a universal solution.
