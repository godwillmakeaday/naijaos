# Earning from Nigeria by Creating Value for the World

By Paul A. Magaji

Editorial context: No international earnings, paid customers or active affiliate partnership are claimed.

I want an international presence because useful work should have the opportunity to travel. Knowledge, judgment or a practical skill may address a need beyond my immediate environment.

The challenge is making that value visible, understandable and purchasable.

I am exploring the commercial possibility. This is a direction I want to pursue, rather than a claim that international income has already followed.

## Begin with a recognised problem

My interests provide subjects. A buyer needs a reason to spend money.

What are they trying to accomplish? Where are they struggling? What would a useful result look like?

An article can investigate the question. A practical resource can demonstrate an approach. A conversation with a potential buyer can reveal whether the difficulty is important enough to justify payment.

I want an offer to emerge from that understanding.

## Make the deliverable clear

“Insight” is difficult to assess before purchase. A defined research brief, workshop or resource gives someone a more concrete basis for consideration.

The description should explain what they receive, what information it depends on and what requires their participation. Outcomes outside my control need clear limits.

My legal background makes me attentive to those boundaries. They help both sides understand the engagement.

## Demonstrate capability publicly

A website allows someone to examine my thinking before making an enquiry. An essay can show care with evidence. A project can show how I approach a problem. A worksheet can let someone try part of a method.

These examples do not guarantee a sale. They give a buyer a basis for considering one.

My phrase “global-rate value” means work that can justify a fair price in a wider market. Visibility alone does not establish that price. Scope, costs, expertise, alternatives and dependable delivery all matter.

## Treat referrals as a specific opportunity

Some tools may offer affiliate programmes. Where an approved relationship exists, a useful account could include a clearly disclosed referral link.

The experience must support the recommendation. I need to explain its circumstances and limitations, and check the current programme terms before treating it as an opportunity.

This collection currently contains no affiliate purchase links and does not announce a sponsored partnership. Any later relationship should be disclosed beside the relevant recommendation.

## Deliver what was agreed

Attracting a buyer creates an obligation. Deadlines, revisions and support need clear expectations. The offer should fit what I can realistically provide.

The transaction also needs an understandable route: identity, scope, price, payment and what happens afterwards. The particular arrangements and obligations require checking for the actual engagement.

An initial delivery can teach me whether the offer was clear, useful and sustainable to repeat.

## A first offer brief

Write down the buyer, problem, deliverable, scope limits, delivery time, required inputs and full cost to fulfil. Then discuss the problem with a potential buyer before building a large commercial system around it.
