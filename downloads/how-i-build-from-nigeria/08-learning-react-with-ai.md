# Why I Am Learning React While Building with AI

By Paul A. Magaji

Editorial context: A learning goal, not a claim of React proficiency or a technical tutorial.

The more I use AI to help build websites, the more interested I become in understanding the work underneath.

If I can describe a page and receive help producing it, why spend time learning the technology? Because I want to direct a project, assess changes and maintain what I publish.

React has become one of the subjects I want to learn for that purpose. I am approaching it with projects to develop and questions to answer.

## Building exposes the gaps

I can explain what I want visitors to see and do. Implementation introduces questions I cannot always answer: what is a component, where does information come from and why does one adjustment affect several pages?

Isolated definitions help, but I want to recognise the relationships within an actual project. Then I can follow the consequences of a proposed change.

That understanding should make technical decisions less mysterious.

## Give better instructions

Suppose someone is completing a worksheet. What happens after they enter information? Should the entries remain when they move elsewhere? What should happen when a field is blank?

These begin as questions about the user. Technical learning helps me describe the expected behaviour more precisely and assess whether the implementation matches it.

I also want to understand repeated elements. If navigation or article cards appear across several pages, where should a shared improvement happen? How can a necessary difference fit without making maintenance confusing?

## Improve judgment without overstating competence

Quickly produced code can be tempting to judge by its visible result. A convincing page may still behave poorly under different conditions.

A little React knowledge will not make me capable of finding every weakness. It can help me ask more informed questions and recognise when I need specialist assistance.

Can I explain what this part does? Do I understand the reason for the change? What check would show that the behaviour is correct?

## Keep the learning goal manageable

I could collect courses for a long time without becoming more capable. I want to connect study with a particular need.

A repeated page element gives me a reason to study organisation. An interactive worksheet gives me questions about inputs and changing information. If the difficulty comes from JavaScript basics, I need to address that gap rather than assume another framework lesson will settle it.

The project gives the lesson somewhere to go.

## Learn to maintain

Launching creates continuing work. I want to locate relevant material, describe a failure and understand the scope of a correction. Recognising the limit of my understanding is part of that capability.

My immediate goal is to open a project, recognise its main parts and explain a small feature. From there, I can develop further.

## A practical learning check

Choose a small, separate exercise. Explain its expected behaviour, make one limited change and inspect the result. Record why it behaved as it did. Keep learning experiments apart from changes to a public website.
