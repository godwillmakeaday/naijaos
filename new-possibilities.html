The founding argument

A country can become better at making things possible.

NaijaOS examines how Nigeria works—and how it could work differently. This programme follows new possibilities from imagination through evidence, engineering, enterprise, law and public life.

A promising idea may fail because the science does not hold. It may also fail because nobody can finance it, a contract allocates risk badly, users cannot trust it, or the institution meant to support it cannot function. Our task is to identify which barrier actually matters.

AI is an opening investigation. Materials, energy, computing, education, markets and public institutions belong within the same inquiry. Each needs its own expertise and evidence.

Our proposed mission: help people and institutions understand emerging possibilities, test workable applications and design the conditions for responsible adoption.

Three programmes / one connected inquiry

01 / UNDERSTAND

Make the change legible.

Explain what an innovation does, how it works and what follows if it is adopted.

02 / CREATE

Test a possibility.

Turn an attractive idea into a bounded experiment with an observable outcome.

  • Possibility Lab worksheets

  • Costs, failures and conditions for repeating a result
03 / GOVERN

Build the conditions.

Examine the rights, responsibilities and institutions an innovation needs.

  • Adoption and procurement questions
  • Ownership, accountability and remedies
  • Institutional design and public-interest analysis

FIRST STUDY · PROPOSED / NO RESULTS YET

When does AI become useful to a small Nigerian business?

Starting question. Can existing traders and service providers in Lafia use AI to prepare sales materials that they can verify and use at an acceptable total cost?

Proposed design. Recruit 10 consenting businesses. Record their usual process, then compare it with an AI-assisted version of the same task. Record human corrections, time, data and subscription costs.

Evidence to collect. Usable outputs, factual errors, owner approval, time saved and full costs. Track enquiries and paid orders separately; changes in sales alone would not establish that AI caused them.

Before fieldwork. Confirm consent, minimise personal data, define the scoring rubric and recruit a technical reviewer. Stop tasks that expose confidential information or generate claims the business cannot verify.

Deliverable: a methods note, an anonymised findings brief and a practical adoption checklist. Recruitment, testing and independent review remain to be done.

How authority will be earned

Make the work inspectable.

Evidence before prestige

Every substantive investigation should distinguish demonstrated facts, interpretation and prediction. Publish sources, methods, dates, limitations and corrections. A professional title creates an obligation to investigate carefully.

Geographical honesty

A finding from Lafia describes the participants and conditions tested. Broader claims require broader evidence, country-specific collaborators and attention to language, infrastructure and institutions.

Independence and funding

The proposed model combines public explanations and research with paid workshops and institutional assessments. Disclose funders and conflicts on each relevant publication. Sponsors must not purchase conclusions or suppress unfavourable findings.

Expertise and accountability

The founder sets research questions and carries editorial accountability. Technical specialists review technical claims; field researchers document observations. Legal conclusions require jurisdiction-specific verification. External reviewers should be identified only after they have accepted the role.

Institutional status

This page is a founding proposal for a programme within NaijaOS. It does not claim a separately registered NGO, university affiliation, appointed research team, active recruitment or completed studies. Organisational form and funding arrangements remain decisions to resolve as the work develops.

First 90 days / proposed workplan

Build a body of work.

DAYS 01–30

Establish the questions.

Refine the founding essay. Prepare three City of AI explainers. Finalise the study rubric, consent process and reviewer roles.

DAYS 31–60

Run a bounded test.

Recruit consenting businesses, document a baseline and carry out the agreed comparison. Keep a record of failures and corrections.

DAYS 61–90

Publish and challenge.

Release an anonymised findings brief with limitations. Invite methodological criticism. Revise the instrument and decide whether a second study is justified.
