The Foundations of Practice Inquiry

By Paul A. Magaji

What must we understand, justify and build for a better practice to become possible?

Better for whom, by what standard, and how would we know?

Use this six-step worksheet for one specific practice in a business, profession, household, community or public institution.

From dissatisfaction to a defensible project

Practices carry assumptions about reality, knowledge, value and human behaviour. Intellectual work makes those assumptions explicit, tests them and helps people construct alternatives. Experience and the knowledge of practitioners must be able to correct the theory.

Understanding why something exists does not establish that it deserves to continue. A successful practice can still be unjust; a worthy proposal can still be unworkable.

This is a working framework, not a validated assessment or scoring system. Begin with a small inquiry and revise it when evidence challenges your explanation.

1. Describe the practice 2. Reveal its foundations 3. Explain its persistence 4. Define improvement 5. Construct an alternative 6. Test and revise

Your inquiry
1. Describe the practice

Describe observable actions before judging them.

2. Reveal its foundations

Make the assumptions and practical conditions visible.

3. Explain its persistence

Changing a belief may leave the incentives and constraints untouched.

4. Define improvement

Better for whom, by what standard, and over what period?

5. Construct an alternative

Identify what we must understand, justify and build.

6. Test and revise

Let evidence and affected people correct the proposal.

Decision record

Working discipline: Treat the explanation of why a practice exists separately from the judgment of whether it deserves to continue. Let evidence—and the people affected—correct the proposal.
